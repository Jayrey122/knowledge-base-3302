# Intrinsic and Extrinsic Motivation

Students can be motivated for different reasons. Intrinsic motivation happens when someone is interested in an activity because it feels enjoyable, meaningful, or challenging. Extrinsic motivation happens when someone works toward an outside rewards like points, a badge, a prize, or recognition.

## Intrinsic Motivation

Intrinsic motivation comes from inside the person. A student may want to solve a challenge because they enjoy learning new thinks or feel proud when they improve. This kind of motivation can help students stay interested when when an activity is difficult because the reward comes from making progress and understanding something new. 

## Extrinsic Motivation

Extrinsic motivation comes from outside rewards. Points, badges, and leaderboards are examples of extrinsic motivation. These tools can encourage students to participate especially when they are first learning a new skill or need extra encouragement to continue.

## Finding the Balance

Gamification should not depend only on rewards. If students only care about earning points they may stop participating when rewards are gone. A strong activity uses rewards to support interest, curiosity, effort, and personal growth instead of replacing those things.
