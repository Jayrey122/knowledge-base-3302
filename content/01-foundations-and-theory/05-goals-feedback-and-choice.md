# Goals, Feedback, and Choice

Goals, feedback, and choice are three important parts of a successful gamified activity. Students need to understand what they are working toward, receive useful information while they work, and have some control over how they participate.

This image highlights the three design elements of gamification: goals, feedback, and choice.

![A three part gamification graphic with a target for goals, a checkmark for feedback, and a branching path for choices](goals-feedback-and-choice.jpg)

## Goals Give Direction

A clear goal tells students what they need to accomplish. It may involve finishing a challenge, reaching a level, solving a problem, or demonstrating a skill. Goals make activities feel more organized because students know what success looks like and can focus on the next step.

## Feedback Supports Improvement

Feedback tells students how they are doing while they are still working. It can explain what was correct, what needs to be improved, and what step to try next. Helpful feedback gives students a chance to learn from mistakes instead of only seeing that an answer was wrong. 

## Choice gives Students Control

Choice can make students feel more involved in an activity. Students may choose the order of challenges, the role they take in a group, or the way they complete a task. Choice does not mean there are no rules it means students have some control within the activity.
