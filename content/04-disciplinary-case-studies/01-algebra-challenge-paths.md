# Algebra Challenge Paths

Algebra challenge paths can turn equation solving into a series of smaller goals. Students may begin with simple linear equations and more forward after showing that they understand each step. A digital graphing simulation can help students check their equations and see how their work connects to a graph.

This image shows an algebra challenge path with a coordinate grid, linear equations, checkpoints, and a goal at the end.

![A coordinate grid showing linear equations, challenge checkpoints, and a final algebra goal](algebra-challenge-path.jpg)

## Moving Through the Challenge

A challenge path can begin with solving basic equations and then move toward more difficult problems. Students may unlock the next checkpoint after they solve an equation, explain their steps, or match the equation to the correct graph. The purpose is to help students make progress without feeling overwhelmed by a long assignment. 

## Using Graphs to Check Work

A graphing simulation can give students another way to see whether their answer makes sense. For example students can graph both sides of an equation and look for the point where they meet. This can help students understand that solving an equation means finding a value that makes both sides equal.

## Learning From Mistakes

Students may make an error while solving an equation, but the challenge can encourage them to revise their work. Instead of losing all of their progress they can use feedback, fix the issue, and continue to the next part of the path.

> An algebra challenge should reward students for explaining their thinking and improve their work not only for getting the answer first.
