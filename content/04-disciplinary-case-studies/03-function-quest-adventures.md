# Function Quest Adventures

Function quest adventures use game like levels to help students explore functions and transformations. Students can begin with a parent function and complete challenges that ask them to move, stretch, shrink, or reflect a graph. A graphing simulation gives students immediate visual feedback as they test different equations.

## Starting with Parent Function

Students can begin the quest by identifying the basic shape of a parent function. They might work with linear, quadratic, absolute value, or exponential functions. The first level can ask students to describe the graph before they make any changes.

## Unlocking Transfomation

As students move through the quest they can change numbers in the equation and watch the graph respond. A level might ask them to move a parabola up, shift it left, make it narrower, or reflect it across an axis. Students can earn progress after they correctly explain what part of the equation caused the change.

## Creating a Final Graph

The final quest can ask students to create an equation that matches a target graph. Students need to use what they learned from earlier levels to decide which transformations are needed. This makes the final challenge feel like a problem solving task instead of a memorization activity.
