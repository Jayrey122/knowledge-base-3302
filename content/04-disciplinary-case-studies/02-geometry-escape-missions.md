# Geometry Escape Missions

Geometry escape missions use puzzles, clues, and digital geometry tools to make geometry feel more active. Students can work through a series of problems to unlock a final answer. Each clue requires students to use angle relationships, triangle rules, area, perimeter, or coordinate geometry.

## Solving Clues With Geometry

Each part of an escape mission can focus on a different skill. Students might need to find a missing angle, identify a congruent shape, calculate an area, or use a coordinate grid. Solving one clue gives them information needed for the next clue.

## Using Dynamic Geometry Tools

A simulation tool can allow students to drag points and test ideas about shapes. They can move a triangle, change a side length, or measure angles while looking for a pattern. This gives students a way to investigate before they explain the rule they discovered.

## Working as a team

Escape missions can work well in groups because students can share strategies. One student can focus on measuring angles while another can look for patterns or check calculations. The group can discuss each clue before deciding on an answer. 

> A geometry puzzle becomes more meaningful when students explain why a relationship is true instead of only finding a code to unlock.
