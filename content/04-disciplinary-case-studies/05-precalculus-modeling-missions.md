# Precalculus Modeling Missions

Precalculus modeling missions use real world situations to help students explore advanced functions. Students can use graphing simulations to model patterns such as population growth, waves, motion, finance, or the shape of a roller coaster. A mission gives students a goal and asks them to use equations and graphs to create a model that fits the situation.

## Modeling a Situation

Students might be given a real world situation and asked to decide which type of function fits best. A roller coaster may be modeled with a quadratic or polynomial function while a repeating wave may be modeled with a sine or cosine function. A graphing simulation helps students compare their model to the situation.

## Testing and Revising

A mission can ask students to adjust their equation until the graph meets certain conditions. They may need to change the amplitude, period, growth rate, or starting value. Students can test different values and see whether their graph becomes a better model.

## Explaining the Model

The final part of the mission is explaining why the equation fits the situation. Students should describe what the number in the equation mean and how the graph represents the pattern. This helps students connect the math to something beyond the screen.

> A strong Mathematical model is not only a graph that looks correct. It is also an explanation of what the graph means.
