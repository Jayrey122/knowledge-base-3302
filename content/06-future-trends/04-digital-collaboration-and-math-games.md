# Digital Collaboration and Math Games

Future math learning can include more digital activities that allow students to work together from the same classroom or from different locations. Students can share graphs, solve challenges as a team, give one another feedback, and work towards a common goal using online math tools.

![Students collaborating on a shared digital math challenge with graphs, team icons, and progress markers](digital-collaboration-and-math-games.jpg)

## Working Toward a Shared Goal

A digital math game does not always need to rank students against one another. Students can work in teams to solve problems, complete a set of levels, or reach a class goal. This can make gamification feel more supportive because students are encourages to help each other.

## Sharing Math Ideas

Digital tools can allow students to see a partner's graph, model, or explanation in real time. One student might notice a mistake and another might suggest a different strategy. These conversations can help students understand that there can be more than one way to approach a math problem.

## Using Competition Carefully

Competition can add energy to a math game, but it should not make students feel embarrassed or left behind. Future activities can use team missions, personal progress, and shared achievements instead of only showing public rankings.
