# Ethics, Privacy, and Fairness

As more math activities use digital tools, simulations, and artificial intelligence schools need to think carefully about privacy and fairness. These systems may collect information about student progress, answers, time spent on activities, and the types of support students need. That information should be protected and used responsibly.

## Protecting Student Information

Students and families should understand what information a digital tool collects and why it is needed. Schools should use tools that protect student data and avoid collecting information that is not necessary for learning. Student information should not be shared without a clear reason.

## Keeping Technology Fair

Technology should give every student a fair chance to learn. A gamified system should not favor students who have faster internet, newer devices, or more experienced with technology. AI feedback should also be checked to make sure it is accurate and does not create unfair assumptions about students. 

## Making Responsible Choices

Teachers and should should decide whether a tool truly supports math learning before using it. A digital activity should have clear goals, protect student privacy, and give students useful support. technology is most helpful when it respects students and helps them grow.

### Before using a digital math tool schools should consider:

- Whether the tool protects student information.
- Whether every student can access the activity.
- Whether the game like features support math learning.
- Whether teachers can guide students when they need help.

> The future of learning should be more than advanced technology. It should also be fair, safe, and helpful for every student.
