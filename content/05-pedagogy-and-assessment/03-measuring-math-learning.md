# Measuring Math Learning

Points, badges, and completed levels can show progress in a gamified activity, but they do not always prove that students understand the math. measuring math learning means looking at whether students can solve problems, explain their thinking, use a graph or model, and apply a skill in a new situation.

## Looking Beyond Points

A student may earn a high score by completing many tasks, but still need help explaining the math. Teachers can use written work, short quizzes, discussions, graphing tasks, or projects to see what students understand. These forms of assessment show more than a score alone.

## Checking Learning During an Activity

Teachers can also check learning while students are completing a challenge. They can look at student graphs, ask questions, listen to group discussions, or review a student's explanation. Digital activities can make it easier to see student work in real time and decide who needs extra support.

## Asking Students to Explain

An explanation can show whether a student understands a result. For example students may be asked why a graph moved, why an equation has a certain solution, or why a data set has an outlier. This makes assessment more focused on reasoning.

> Finishing a level shows progress, but explaining the math shows understanding.
