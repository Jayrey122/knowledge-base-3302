# Designing Gamified Math Lessons

Designing a gamified math lesson begins with a learning goal. Before adding points, badges, levels, or rewards, a teach should decide what students need to understand. The game like parts of the lesson should help students explore a math idea, practice a skill, or explain their thinking.

## Starting With the Math Goal

A teacher might want students to understand slope, solve equations, compare data, or describe a transformation. That goal should come first. The challenge, simulation, or reward should be chosen because it helps students work toward a goal.

## Using Game Elements Carefully

A graphing activity may include levels that move from simple equations to harder ones. A geometry activity may use a mission that asks students to create a shape with certain measurements. The features can make the lesson feel more active, but they should not distract students from the mathematics.

## Planning for Reflection

Students should have time to explain what they learned after completing a challenge. They can write a short explanation, discuss their strategy with a partner, or describe how a graph changed. 

> A gamified math lesson should begin with a learning goal not with a reward.
