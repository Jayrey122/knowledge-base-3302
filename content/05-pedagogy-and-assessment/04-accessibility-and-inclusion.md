# Accessibility and Inclusion

Gamified math activities should give every student a fair chance to participate. Students learn in different ways, work at different speeds, and may have different access to technology. A thoughtful activity gives students support without making anyone feel left out.

This image shows students using different tools and support during an inclusive math activity. 

![A diverse group of students using laptops, printed graphs, calculators, and accessibility supports during a math activity](accessibility-and-inclusion.jpg)

## Different ways to Participate

Some students may prefer working independently while others might enjoy working with a partner or group. Some might need printed directions, captions, extra time, a screen reader, or access to a school device. Teachers can plan different ways for students to take part in the same math challenge.

## Competition and Fairness

Leaderboards and times challenges can motivate some students, but they can also create pressure. A student who needs more time might feel discouraged by a public ranking. 

## Supporting Every Student

A fair activity recognizes effort, reasoning, creativity, and improvement. Students should have more than one way to succeed. This makes gamification feel more supportive and less like a competition that only a few students can win.

> Good gamification gives students a path to improve instead of making them feel behind.
