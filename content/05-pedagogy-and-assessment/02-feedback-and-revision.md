# Feedback and Revision

Feedback is important in gamified math activities because students need to know what they are doing well and what they need to improve. A simulation can show students how a graph changes while feedback can help them understand why their answer worked or did not work.

This image shows a math graphing activity with feedback, an incorrect attempt, and a revised answer.

![A digital math activity showing an incorrect graph, feedback message, and a revised correct graph](feedback-and-revision.jpg)

## Feedback During Learning

Students should receive feedback while they are still working not only after the activity is over. A graphing tool may show that a student's line does not match the target while a teacher can ask the student to explain what needs to change. This gives students a chance to learn from the mistake.

## Revision Is Part of Learning

Gamification can make revision feel like part of the challenge. Students can earn progress after they correct an error, explain a new strategy, or improve an earlier answer. This can help students see that mistakes are not the end of the activity.

## Avoiding Guessing

Students should not only click answers until they find the correct one. Feedback should encourage them to think about why an answer is wrong and what they can try next. This helps students build stronger math reasoning.

### A useful feedback process includes:

- Noticing what does not match.
- Explaining what needs to change.
- Revising the work.
- Checking whether the new answer makes sense.
