# Reflection and Student Voice

Reflection gives students time to think about what they learned during a gamified math activity. After completing a challenge or simulation students can explain the strategy they used, describe a mistake they corrected, or share what part of the math was difficult. Student voice is important because it gives teachers information about how students experienced the activity.

## Thinking About the Process

Students may be excited about earning points or reaching a new level, but reflection helps them focus on the math. A teacher can ask students what they noticed in a graphing simulation, how they solved a problem, or what they would do differently next time.

## Learning From Other Students

Students can also learn by hearing different strategies. One student can explain how they changed an equation while another can describe how they used a graph to check their answer. These conversations can help students see that there is often more than one way to approach a math problem.

## Using Student Feedback

Teachers can ask students whether the challenge was clear, whether the simulation helped them understand, and what part of the activity was confusing. This feedback can help improve future lessons. When students feel heard they may feel more connected to the learning experience. A reflection can be short. Students can write one sentence about what they learned, one question they still have, and one strategy that helped them complete the activity.
