# Geometry Missions

Geometry missions use dynamic geometry simulations and gamification to make shapes, angles, and measurements more interactive. Students can move points, change side lengths, and measure angles while working toward a goal. A mission may ask students to create a specific shape to prove that a relationship stays true or solve a design problem using geometry. 

This image shows a geometry mission with a movable triable, angle measurements, a challenge goal, and a Geometry Explorer Badge.

![A digital geometry mission showing a movable triable, measured angles, a challenge goal, and a Geometry Explorer badge](geometry-missions.jpg)

## Building and Moving Shapes

Dynamic geometry tools let students create triangles, circles, polygons, and other shapes. Students can drag points and watch what happens to the angles, side lengths, area, or perimeter in real time. This makes geometry feel more active because the diagram is not fixed.

## Completing a Mission

A gamified geometry mission gives students a clear reason to use the tool. For example a student may be asked to create a triangle with certain measurements or show that the angles always add up to the same total. After completing the mission students can earn a badge or unlock a harder shape challenge.

## Testing Conjectures

Students can make a prediction and then test it by moving parts of the shape. They may notice that some features change while other rules stay the same. This gives students evidence they can use when writing an explanation or proof. 

> Moving a shape can change its appearance, but it does not change the mathematical relationships that define it.
