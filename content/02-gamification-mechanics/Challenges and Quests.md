
# Challenges and Quests

Challenges and quests give gamified activities and assignments a purpose. A challenge asks users to complete a goal. A quest often adds a story, theme, or series of connected tasks. These elements can make an activity feel more like an adventure than a simple checklist.

![[gamification-mathematics.pdf]]
## Clear Goals

A successful challenge has a goal that students can understand. It might ask them to solve a problem, complete a task, or reach a certain level of skill. Clear instructions help students know what they need to of and how success will be measured.

## The Role of a Story

A quest can include a story that gives meaning to the task. For example, students may be asked to become explorers, detectives, team leaders, or inventors. The story does not need to be complicated. Its purpose is to make the activity more interesting and create a reason for students to continue.

## Choice and Participation

Challenges can be more motivating when students have choices. They may choose which quest to complete first, select a role, or decide how to solve a problem. Choice can give  users a greater sense of control and make their participation feel more personal.

> A good quest gives people a goal, a reason to continue, and an opportunity to make decisions.
